Meet the Lab

We’re looking for passionate graduate students, undergraduates, and postdoctoral fellows to join our team — see our Join page!

Mike Piacentino, PhD

he/him/his | PI
michaelpiacentino [at] jhmi [dot] edu

Mike is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Cell Biology at the Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine. Mike received his PhD from Boston University where he studied skeletal patterning in sea urchin embryos. He fell in love with neural crest cells and chick embryology as a postdoc with Marianne Bronner at Caltech, sparking his interest in lipid metabolism during cell migration.

Mike can’t wait to unlock the mysteries of the neural crest cell membrane. Outside of lab, Mike enjoys going on hikes, mastering new recipes in the kitchen, and fiber crafts like knitting and crochet!

Allison Mancini, PhD

she/her/her | Postdoctoral Fellow

Alli earned her PhD in Molecular Medicine at the University of Maryland School of Medicine working under Dr. Megan Rizzo. During her doctoral work she developed novel fluorescent biosensors, determined how calcium activates RhoA and Rho-kinase in fibroblasts, and characterized the role of Rho-kinase in collective cell migration. In addition to being deeply passionate about microscopy, Alli is extremely interested in the biophysics of cell migration. She is very excited to start learning about the role of lipids in neural crest development. Outside of the lab she enjoys reading, knitting, live music, and travel.

Mallorie Jenne

she/her/her | BCMB PhD Candidate

Mallorie is a PhD candidate in the Biochemistry, Cellular and Molecular Biology program. She earned her BS in Chemistry and Biology from Harvey Mudd College. She previously worked in a research lab under Dr. Jae Hur studying the role of the gene CG5815 in lipid metabolism in fruit flies. She is excited to continue studying lipids in the Piacentino Lab. Outside of lab, Mallorie enjoys running, hiking, and looking at plants.

Stephanie Peralta

she/her/hers | BCMB PhD Student
speralt1 [at] jhu [dot] edu

Stephanie obtained her BS in Health Science at Mercy College, where she discovered a passion for being in a laboratory atmosphere. During her undergraduate years, she worked in Dr. Anthony Canger’s lab, and her research focused on paxillin and actin expression during neural crest cell migration in chick embryos and retinoic acid as a teratogen in chick CNS development. Stephanie obtained her MS in Biotechnology with a concentration in Biodefense at Johns Hopkins University. She is very excited to start working with chick embryos again and circle back to what made her initially fall in love with working in a laboratory. Outside of the lab, Stephanie enjoys cooking, hiking, reading, and watching sunsets.

Tristan Oper

he/him/his | Research Technologist

Tristan obtained a BS in Biochemistry and Psychology from the University of New Mexico where he developed a passion for biomedical research while working in a lab under Dr. Curt Hines. Tristan spent four years investigating the role of pericytes in maintaining breast tissue homeostasis and their contributions to tumor development. Tristan is excited to explore more about how lipid organization influences EMT within the neural crest. Outside of the lab, Tristan enjoys many hobbies including video editing, chess, dungeons and dragons, and skiing.
